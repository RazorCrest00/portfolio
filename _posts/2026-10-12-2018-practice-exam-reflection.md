**Practice exam score: 63/66 (95.5%)**

Taking the 2018 Practice Exam MCQ let me apply what I had learned while working productively with my team. Everyone contributed, and I played an active part by explaining my reasoning and listening to my teammates' approaches. We helped each other understand the material while still thinking through our own answers. When unfamiliar vocabulary interrupted my understanding, I used Google to look up the terms, then applied the explanation myself. I did not search or copy and paste the actual questions. Google helped me understand what was being asked; the reasoning behind my answer still had to be my own.

## Test strengths and weaknesses

**My test strengths:** I earned 100% in **Binary Numbers, Data Compression, Extracting Information from Data, and Developing Algorithms**, as well as Boolean Expressions, Conditionals, and Iteration. These results suggest a solid foundation in representing information and following program logic. Extracting Information from Data was especially encouraging because it covered seven items, giving me more evidence of consistency than a section assessed only once. Strong results in The Internet and Safe Computing showed breadth beyond programming. Sections marked N/A were not assessed, so I cannot count them as demonstrated strengths.

**My test weaknesses:** I was less consistent in **Using Programs with Data (50%)**, **Identifying and Correcting Errors (75%)**, and **Calling Procedures (80%)**. The contrast with my data-interpretation result suggests I should practice explaining how programs process information. For debugging and procedure calls, I need to check how carefully I track changing values, arguments, and returned results. These are possible gaps to investigate; the breakdown alone does not establish their causes. The data-use section contained only two items, so its percentage identifies a priority without fully defining my ability.

## Strengths and weaknesses in class

**My classroom strengths:** I contribute to my team's learning by explaining my thinking, listening to other approaches, and working through uncertainty with others. That exchange helps me put an idea into my own words and notice where my explanation is incomplete. I also use resources purposefully when I encounter unfamiliar terms. These habits support both my own understanding and our shared work. My next opportunity is to bring that same curiosity and teamwork to improving the project after the day's assigned task is finished.

**My classroom weakness:** When I finish assigned work, I sometimes go off task, such as looking at sports, even though I could keep improving our project. The cost is the testing, refinement, or teammate support I could have contributed during that time. I need to treat finishing an assignment as a checkpoint for choosing the next useful task. For the next five classes, I will keep sports tabs closed during work time, choose one manageable project improvement when my assigned work is done, and record what I completed before leaving. That could mean testing an edge case, fixing a bug, improving an explanation, or helping a teammate resolve a blocker.

## My next steps

For my next review, I plan to use AI for **Using Programs with Data** and **Calling Procedures**. I will request fresh examples with solutions withheld, work through them independently, and then ask for feedback on my explanation. For debugging, I will compare expected and actual behavior to locate the first difference. Over the next week, I will complete five new problems in each weaker section, aiming for at least four correct in each without help, and explain one solution from each section to my team. A repeat check a few days later will test retention; my classroom notes will show whether I also used my remaining work time productively.
